Sources identify records differently. The package preserves the fields needed for identity instead of reducing every instrument to one code.

## Securities and companies

Listed, OTC and emerging security codes are strings, preserving leading zeros such as `0050`. Source namespaces distinguish markets; directories also retain ISIN, classification and listing dates. Company statements, revenue and shareholding queries use company or security codes while preserving their own periods and provenance.

## Futures and options

Contracts include product and delivery month or weekly series. Options also require strike and right; spreads require both leg periods. A product code alone cannot identify a quote.

Active listings and `resolve()` check MIS IDs against official metadata and retain explicit expiration dates. Report and quote identifiers can differ; mappings are verified against official listings. Spread legs retain source periods without synthesizing MIS IDs or expiration dates. An adjusted contract's multiplier remains `None` when its individual share count is unpublished.

## Economic series and currencies

Economic observations are identified by series and month, so unrelated values cannot be merged on month alone. Exchange rates are identified by pair and date. A `USD` filter can match either side of the published pair without changing its direction.

## Batch inputs

Batch queries retain input order and duplicates. Deduplicating network requests does not deduplicate results; each outcome still corresponds to its requested input and individual error. See [batch queries](/en/docs/how-to/query) and [models](/en/docs/reference/api/models).
